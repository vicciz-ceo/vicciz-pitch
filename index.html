vicciz · Connected trip infrastructure

The AI agent that re-coordinates the whole trip when travel breaks

Every disruption tool on the market fixes the one thing its owner sold. Kiipling fixes everything else the delay just broke — across airlines, hotels, transfers and tours, without a single integration.

vicciz is a travel-technology company building Kiipling, an AI agent that manages a trip after it is booked. When a flight is delayed or cancelled, Kiipling detects the disruption automatically, works out which hotel, ground transport, tour and activity bookings are about to break, drafts a specific fix for each affected provider, and executes every one of them the moment the traveller approves it. It reaches providers on the channels they already answer — WhatsApp and email — so it needs no API integration with the hotel or the car-rental desk.

vicciz sells Kiipling primarily to businesses: online travel agencies, travel agents and tour operators buy it as white-label trip care for their own customers, and the agent signs its messages in their name. The product is also available directly to travellers through the vicciz app. Every provider-facing action requires explicit human approval before it is sent — a deliberate design choice that keeps legal responsibility with the person who holds it. The company is based in Israel and runs entirely on EU infrastructure.

The problem

A trip is assembled from many vendors. It breaks as one.

A traveller books the flight through one site, the hotel through another, the transfer through a third and the tour directly with an operator. When the flight slips two hours, all four bookings are suddenly wrong — but no one company is responsible for more than its own line item.

The tools that exist today inherit that boundary. Airline systems re-accommodate passengers onto other flights. Online travel agency servicing engines automate cancellations, exchanges and refunds — for flights. Insurance and claims products pay out after the fact rather than fixing anything. Behind all of them sits a human support layer that costs roughly $25 per contact and is the first budget line every large travel seller is now trying to shrink.

Nobody re-coordinates the rest of the trip. That is the gap vicciz was built to fill.

The product

Four things Kiipling does that adjacent tools do not

Cross-vendor incident coordination

A delayed flight, a late hotel check-in and a missed transfer are handled as one incident with one timeline, not three unrelated support tickets. The agent reasons about downstream impact — which pickup window moved, which check-in is now outside policy, which tour slot is unreachable.

No integration required

Kiipling contacts providers over WhatsApp and email, the channels they already staff. A traveller can be onboarded from a reservation number and a date, or from a forwarded booking confirmation the agent parses itself. No GDS connection, no provider API, no passwords.

Approval as liability architecture

Nothing reaches a provider without explicit human approval. Competitors treat human-in-the-loop as a user-experience choice or a limitation to remove. For a business acting on a customer's behalf it is the mechanism that keeps authority, and legal exposure, with the party that actually holds it.

Priced per resolved incident

A published absolute price per fixed disruption, rather than a percentage of claimed savings or an opaque enterprise contract. It is directly comparable to the cost of the human contact it replaces, which is the number a travel seller is already measuring.

How it works

From detection to executed fix, in five stages

  1. 1

    Monitor

    Disruption monitors poll flight status and other trip signals every 30 minutes against the traveller's booked itinerary, whether or not the trip was booked through a vicciz customer.

  2. 2

    Interpret

    A three-stage AI pipeline classifies the incident and identifies each downstream booking at risk, ranked by how soon it breaks and how hard it is to recover.

  3. 3

    Draft

    The agent writes a specific message to each affected provider, in the traveller's language, informed by the provider's terms and by applicable passenger-rights rules such as EU261 and US DOT regulations.

  4. 4

    Approve

    The traveller — or the agency operator acting for them — sees exactly what will be sent and approves each action individually. A second review stage checks every draft before it is offered.

  5. 5

    Execute and report

    Approved messages go out over WhatsApp and email, replies are threaded back into the same incident, sentiment is monitored for escalation, and the business receives a resolution report for every case.

Who buys it

Business first, consumer second

1 · Travel businesses Priority

Online travel agencies, travel agents and tour operators buy Kiipling as a managed service for their customers. They push trip data over an API and get a dashboard for monitoring trips, escalation reports and resolution metrics. The agent writes to their customers in their name. Mid-market European operators are the wedge: they feel disruption costs acutely and cannot build this themselves.

2 · Travellers, through the vicciz app

Trip data flows automatically from the app to the agent, with no proof of purchase required, and the agent operates under the vicciz brand on a consumer subscription.

3 · Travellers, directly

Anyone can forward a booking confirmation for the agent to parse, or enter reservation numbers by hand. Whatever can be verified is verified; the rest is flagged for review.

Why now

The cost of human trip care is being cut, everywhere

Large travel sellers have spent the last two years replacing support headcount with automation, and they are publishing the savings. That creates a budget line for anything that resolves a case without a person, and a service gap wherever the automation stops — which, today, is at the edge of whatever the seller sold.

The regulatory backdrop compounds it. EU passenger-rights compensation under EU261 represents an estimated €6.5 billion a year, of which roughly two-thirds goes unclaimed. An agent that already knows the rules can fix the trip and file the claim in the same flow — something neither the claims companies nor the airline tools attempt.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is vicciz?

vicciz is a travel-technology company that builds Kiipling, an AI agent for managing trips after they are booked. Its core capability is re-coordinating every downstream booking — hotel, ground transport, tour, activity — when a flight is delayed or cancelled.

What is the difference between vicciz and Kiipling?

vicciz is the company and the consumer-facing brand. Kiipling is the AI trip agent it builds, and the product travel businesses license as white-label trip care.

How is this different from flight-rebooking automation?

Flight-rebooking tools resolve the flight. Kiipling starts where they stop: it identifies the hotel check-in, transfer pickup and tour booking that the new flight time has broken, and fixes each one with its provider. It works across vendors, including bookings a traveller made somewhere else entirely.

Does Kiipling act without asking the traveller?

No. Every provider-facing action requires explicit human approval before it is sent. The agent detects, decides and drafts autonomously, then waits. This is a deliberate liability architecture, not a temporary limitation.

Does a travel business need to integrate anything?

A business pushes trip data to one API and receives a dashboard. No integration is required with the hotels, transfer companies or tour operators themselves — Kiipling reaches those over WhatsApp and email, the channels they already answer.

What does it cost?

Kiipling is priced per resolved incident, in a target range of $8 to $15 per disrupted trip, set against a human support contact costing roughly $25. Business pricing is a subscription or per-customer agreement depending on volume.

Which channels does the agent use?

WhatsApp and email today, with a voice channel planned. It replies in the traveller's preferred language and respects stored preferences for channel and contact hours.

Where is data hosted?

Entirely in the EU. Application services run on Google Cloud Run in europe-west3 (Frankfurt), with PostgreSQL persistence in eu-central-1.

Is Kiipling available today?

The backend for all four product stages is deployed and running as of August 2026, and the first white-label agency pilot is underway. vicciz is talking to travel businesses about further pilots now.
